# The Steep Club Launch: Campaign Plan

**Brand:** Kettlebird Tea (sample brand)
**Product:** The Steep Club, a monthly tea subscription box
**Channel:** Email only. No paid ads.
**Dates:** Monday 2 November to Sunday 29 November 2026 (4 weeks)

> **Before you use this plan:** the details below in square brackets, like `[price]`, are things we don't know yet. Fill them in before any email is written. All emails are drafts only. Nothing goes to a real address.

---

## 1. Campaign overview

**Campaign name:** *Your First Steep*

**Summary:** A four-week email series that turns Kettlebird's waitlist into the first members of The Steep Club.

**Main goal:** 300 waitlist members start a subscription by 29 November 2026.

That is 15% of a 2,000-person list. It's ambitious but possible, because these people have already raised their hand. We'll watch the numbers each week (see section 7) and change course if we fall behind.

**Other goals:**
- Learn which of the three groups signs up most, so we know who to write for next.
- Build a group of "founding members" who can later tell friends about the club.
- Keep unsubscribes low (under 1% per email).

---

## 2. Target audience

About 2,000 people who joined the waitlist. They already know Kettlebird and want to hear about the club. They are in the **decision** stage, so our job is to help them say yes, not to explain who we are.

| Group | Who they are | What they want | What holds them back |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Tea-curious beginners** | People who like tea but don't know much about it. | To try new teas without feeling lost or wasting money. | "I won't know what to do with loose-leaf tea." "What if I don't like it?" |
| **Gift buyers** | People shopping for someone else. | An easy, thoughtful gift that feels personal. | "Can I send it to someone else?" "Will it arrive in time?" "Can I pick how many months?" |
| **Daily tea drinkers** | People who drink tea every day and know what they like. | Better tea, more variety, and never running out. | "Is it better than what I buy now?" "Is it worth the price?" "Can I skip a month?" |

**Where they are:** their inbox. They signed up to hear from us, so email is the right place to reach them.

**One thing to check first:** do we know which group each person belongs to? If the waitlist form asked, we can split the list right away. If not, Email 2 asks them to pick, and we sort people by what they click (see section 9).

## 3. Key messages

**Core message:** *Good tea, picked for you, at your door every month.*

**Supporting messages:**

| Message | For | Proof we can use |
|---|---|---|
| **"No guesswork. Just good tea."** Every box comes with simple brewing cards, so anyone can make a great cup. | Beginners | A photo or sample of the brewing card. What's inside the first box: `[box contents]`. |
| **"A gift that keeps arriving."** Send it to someone you love, for 3, 6 or 12 months. | Gift buyers | Gift options and delivery dates: `[gift options]`, `[last order date for December delivery]`. |
| **"Your daily cup, but better."** New teas each month from `[where the tea comes from]`, so you never get bored or run out. | Daily drinkers | Tea origins, how much tea is in each box (`[cups per box]`), price per cup. |
| **"Waitlist members go first."** A founding-member offer that's only for the waitlist, for a short time. | Everyone | The offer itself: `[founding offer, e.g. first box at a lower price]`. Deadline: `[offer end date]`. |

**Call to action (every email):** *Join The Steep Club* → `[sign-up page link]`

**Tone:** warm, simple, and calm. Short sentences. Talk like a friend who loves tea, not a shop shouting about a sale.

**Claims we must not make:**
- No health claims. Don't say tea cures, heals, helps you lose weight, boosts your immune system, or treats anything.
- No "best tea in the world" or "the cheapest." Only say what we can show.
- No fake scarcity. If we say "only 100 founding spots," that has to be true.
- No promises about delivery dates we can't keep.

## 4. Channel strategy

We are using **email only**, which fits well. The audience opted in, it costs very little, and every email can link straight to the sign-up page.

| Channel | Why it fits | Format | Effort | Budget |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Email to the whole waitlist** | Everyone hears the big moments: launch, last chance. | Short, friendly emails with one clear button. | Low | Email tool we already use. |
| **Email by group** | Each group has different worries. Targeted emails answer them directly. | One email per group in weeks 2 and 3. | Medium | Same tool. Needs the list split into groups. |
| **Automatic follow-ups** | Catches people who click but don't finish signing up. | One reminder email sent 24 hours after they leave the sign-up page. | Low (set up once) | Only if our email tool supports it. |
| **Welcome email for new members** | Makes new members feel good about joining and helps them share. | Thank-you email with a "tell a friend" note. | Low | Same tool. |

**What we're not doing:** paid ads, social media pushes, or influencers. These are out of scope for this budget.

## 7. Success metrics

**Main measure:** new Steep Club subscriptions from the waitlist. **Target: 300 by 29 November.**

**Weekly checkpoints** (total sign-ups so far):

| By end of | Target | If we're behind |
|---|---|---|
| Week 1 (8 Nov) | 100 | Check the sign-up page works. Try a new subject line for Email 2. |
| Week 2 (15 Nov) | 170 | See which group is lagging and send that group an extra email. |
| Week 3 (22 Nov) | 220 | Push the deadline message harder in week 4. |
| Week 4 (29 Nov) | 300 | — |

Launch weeks usually bring the most sign-ups, then a smaller jump near the deadline. That's why the targets aren't even.

**Other measures:**

| Measure | What it tells us | Where we see it | Healthy sign |
|---|---|---|---|
| Open rate | Are the subject lines working? | Email tool | Steady or rising week to week |
| Click rate | Do people want to know more? | Email tool | Higher on group emails than general ones |
| Sign-ups by email | Which email does the work? | Sign-up page links tagged per email | — |
| Sign-ups by group | Which group joins most? | Email 2 clicks plus sign-up data | — |
| Unsubscribe rate | Are we sending too much? | Email tool | Under 1% per email |

**How to track:** give each email's button its own tracking tag (for example `?src=email3b`) so we know which email led to each sign-up.

**Reporting:** a short check every Monday before that week's first email. A full wrap-up on Tuesday 1 December.

## 9. Risks and how we handle them

| Risk | What could happen | What we do |
|---|---|---|
| **We can't tell the groups apart** | We don't know who is a beginner, gift buyer or daily drinker. | Email 2 asks them to pick. People who don't click get a general version of Email 3. |
| **The waitlist has gone cold** | People joined months ago and forgot about us. Opens are low. | Email 1 reminds them they asked to hear about this. Clean out bounced addresses first. If the week 1 open rate is low, resend Email 1 to non-openers with a new subject line. |
| **Too many emails** | Up to 9 emails in 4 weeks could feel like a lot. | Stop emailing people as soon as they join. Watch unsubscribes. If they go above 1%, drop Email 6. |
| **Sign-ups are behind target** | We're short at a weekly checkpoint. | Use the "if we're behind" steps in section 7. Use the spare budget for a small week 4 bonus. |
| **Gift orders miss the holidays** | Gift buyers worry it won't arrive in time. | Set and share a clear order-by date in Emails 3B and 7. |
